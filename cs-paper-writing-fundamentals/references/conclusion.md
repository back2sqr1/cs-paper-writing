# Conclusion

The conclusion tells the reader what the paper established and what it leaves open. It is not a copy of the abstract.

## Contents
1. **What was shown**: the main result, restated briefly with its scope ("on the benchmarks and settings tested").
2. **What it means**: the implication or insight that goes beyond the numbers.
3. **Limitations**: honest boundaries (assumptions, scale, domains not tested). Put them here or in a dedicated section, but do not omit them.
4. **Future work**: 2-3 specific, credible directions that follow from the limitations. Avoid a laundry list.

## Rules
- Introduce no new claims or results here.
- Match the strength of the claims to the evidence; do not escalate relative to the body.
- Use different words from the abstract; focus on interpretation rather than recap.
- Keep it short: one to three paragraphs in most venues.
- End on substance (what is open, what comes next), not on a generic statement about the importance of the field.

## Check before finishing
- Does every claim here appear, with evidence, earlier in the paper?
- Does it admit the main limitation a reviewer would raise?
