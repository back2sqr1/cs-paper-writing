# Mathematics and Algorithms

## Notation
- Introduce notation only when it will be reused; keep it minimal.
- Define every symbol at first use, including its type or domain (e.g., "$x \in \mathbb{R}^n$ is the robot configuration").
- Keep notation identical across text, equations, figures and pseudocode.
- Consider a short notation table for symbol-heavy papers.
- Do not reuse a symbol for two meanings.

## Equations
- Every displayed equation is part of a sentence and is punctuated accordingly.
- Number only equations you refer to.
- Explain in words what an important equation means before or right after it.
- Give intuition before formalism, and a concrete example after a non-trivial definition.

## Theorems and proofs
- State theorems precisely, with all assumptions. A reader should be able to tell exactly what is and is not covered.
- Sketch the intuition before the proof.
- Put long proofs in the appendix; keep a proof sketch in the main text.
- Do not claim a result is "trivial" or "obvious"; give the one-line reason instead.
- Distinguish what you prove from what you conjecture or only observe empirically.

## Algorithms and pseudocode
- State inputs and outputs at the top.
- Use names consistent with the text.
- Comment non-obvious steps.
- Explain in prose what the algorithm does, why it works, and its complexity.
- Keep pseudocode at the level of ideas; leave implementation detail to the appendix or code.

## Check before finishing
- Is every symbol defined before use?
- Does every equation have a sentence that introduces it?
- Do the theorem's assumptions match the settings used in the experiments?
