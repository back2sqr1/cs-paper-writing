# Figures and Tables

Readers skim figures and tables first. Treat them as core content.

## Choosing
- Figure: when the shape, trend or structure matters.
- Table: when exact values matter.
- Diagram: for pipelines and architectures; keep it clean and legible at print size.

## Captions
- Each figure or table must be understandable from its caption alone: what is shown, the units, the setting, and the takeaway.
- Refer to every figure and table in the text and tell the reader what to notice.

## Figures
- Label axes with quantity and units; use font sizes readable at print size.
- Make lines and markers distinguishable without color (style, marker shape); check grayscale.
- Include variability (error bars, shaded bands) and say what they represent.
- Use consistent colors and names for the same method across all figures.
- Include a teaser/overview figure early if it can convey the idea at a glance; make the pipeline figure match the method's terms.
- Avoid chart junk: heavy grids, 3-D effects, unnecessary legends.

## Tables
- Minimal ink: no vertical rules, few horizontal rules.
- Align decimal points; use consistent precision justified by the data.
- Put the best result in bold only if the difference is meaningful.
- State metric direction (higher or lower is better) in the header or caption.
- Keep column and row labels identical to the terms used in the text.

## Check before finishing
- Could a reader skimming only figures, tables and captions grasp the main results?
- Is each figure referenced and discussed in the text?
- Do numbers in tables match those quoted in the text?
