# Revision Workflow and Self-Review

## Revision passes (in order)
1. **Reverse-outline**: list each paragraph's first sentence; check the sequence makes the argument on its own. Move, merge or cut paragraphs that do not map to the thesis.
2. **Claim check**: verify each claim against its evidence (see `claims-and-evidence.md`).
3. **Terminology and notation**: scan the whole paper for consistent names and symbols.
4. **Cuts**: read once purely for removal. Delete anything that does not serve the research question.
5. **Skeptical read**: read aloud or as a hostile reviewer; fix every place you hesitate.
6. **Figures, tables, captions**: check last, as a skimming reader would.

## Self-review question list (answer each before submission)
**Contribution**
- What exactly is new? Could a reader state it in one sentence?
- Is the closest prior work discussed fairly, and is the difference clear?

**Writing clarity**
- Does every paragraph have one message in its first sentence?
- Is each term defined before use and used consistently?

**Experimental strength**
- Are baselines strong and tuned comparably?
- Are variability and noise reported?

**Evaluation completeness**
- Does every claim have an experiment, and every experiment a question?
- Are ablations, failure cases and limitations included?

**Method soundness**
- Are assumptions explicit?
- Is the method reproducible from the text?

## Output format when revising a draft
1. The revised text.
2. A short list of changes and why.
3. Claims needing evidence, as `Claim: ... | Evidence: ... | Status: supported / needs evidence`.
4. Unresolved items from the self-review list.
