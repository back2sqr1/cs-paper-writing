# Related Work and Background

Related work places your contribution relative to what exists. It is an argument, not a bibliography.

## Organize by idea, not by paper
- Group work into 2-5 themes or families of approach. Give each a short paragraph (or subsection) with a topic sentence naming the theme.
- Within a theme, move from the foundational work to the closest competitors.
- End each theme by stating how your work differs: what it assumes less, guarantees more, handles at larger scale, or does in a different setting.
- Avoid "A did X. B did Y. C did Z." lists. Compare: what do A, B and C share, and where do they diverge?

## Be accurate and fair
- Describe each cited work as its authors would. Do not paraphrase it into a weaker version to make yours look better.
- Cite the original source of an idea, not only the most recent paper that uses it.
- Include competing approaches, especially ones that beat you on some axis. State the trade-off.
- If a baseline in your experiments is described here, make the description match what you ran.

## Background vs related work
- **Background** (preliminaries): material the reader needs to follow your method (definitions, problem formulation, standard algorithms). Keep it minimal and place it before the method.
- **Related work**: alternatives and prior art, positioned against your contribution. Can go after the introduction or before the conclusion; choose the position that lets the reader understand the comparison. If the comparison depends on your method, put it later.

## Rules
- Every paragraph ends in a relation to your work, not just a summary of theirs.
- Use consistent terminology: if you call something "trajectory optimization" in the method, do not rename it when discussing prior work, unless you explain the mapping.
- Do not pad. Cite what the reader needs to place your work; a long list of tangential citations hides the important ones.
- Recent preprints: cite them if relevant, but check whether a peer-reviewed version exists.

## Check before finishing
- For each theme, can you finish the sentence "Unlike these works, we ..." truthfully?
- Is the closest prior work discussed prominently and fairly, not buried?
- Does every cited paper in this section serve a stated purpose?
